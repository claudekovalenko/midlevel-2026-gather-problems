The headline of the whole dataset sits here: every gathering is confident in the Word, and 8 of 9 name multiplication as their growth edge.

What leaders are celebrating

  • Multiplication is starting to happen. Neighbors & Nations has planted two house churches out of its gathering (Compton and La Mirada) and sent out two elders to plant their own church. Nations Church saw a baptism, new people joining, and a new GCT.
  • New believers at the core. Rooted Church (Compton) is a youth house church made up of four new believers who came to faith last year.
  • Real community, not event attendance. Multiple leaders describe consistent Word-centered discipleship, shared meals, serving one another's practical needs, and groups "sold out to Jesus."
  • New works launching. Saints Church (Long Beach) has just begun its first steps; Coach (Inland Empire) is being invited in to coach several groups, including work touching Iranians, North Africans, and Afghans.

The challenges they're carrying

  • Legacy-church gravity — the most repeated theme. "Church as an event to attend rather than an identity" (Garden), members still tied to legacy churches which dilutes multiplication DNA (Anchor Anaheim), and coached young adults feeling obligated to run the legacy church's playbook (Corona Sandals).
  • From fellowship to obedience-based discipleship. Several leaders say their people love Jesus and each other but don't yet practice discipleship as obedience and reproduction.
  • Retention and depth. Baptisms that don't stick (member survey), young believers whose Catholic families won't allow baptism (Rooted), going through the motions and lacking affection (Nations), summer inconsistency (Anchor).
  • Leader bandwidth. Balancing normal shepherding with an eye toward new work; wanting new elders so vision and new places get attention (Neighbors & Nations).
"For too long, we've been exposed to a discipleship of knowledge rather than a discipleship of obedience."Rick Gregory, Garden (Huntington Beach)
